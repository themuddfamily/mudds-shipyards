# Lifecycle-dependent phantom geometry audit

Phase 10 §1 asks for "flashing materials/lights, and lifecycle-dependent phantom
geometry" to be removed. `tests/long_session_soak_test.gd` already drives
production `Main` through real sortie cycles with damage/recovery,
destruction/regeneration and whole-`Main` re-entry — but it counts nodes and
objects, not pixels. The recorded defect this item exists for was a dormant
`HeroDamagePresentation/DamageSparks` renderer that stayed *visible* across
states and filled the screen with a warm flash while every counter sat exactly
where it started.

`tests/lifecycle_phantom_geometry_test.gd` extends that soak and asks the other
question: what is actually being drawn?

```
godot --headless --audio-driver Dummy --path . \
  --script res://tests/lifecycle_phantom_geometry_test.gd
KETH_PHANTOM_CYCLES=12 godot --headless --audio-driver Dummy --path . \
  --script res://tests/lifecycle_phantom_geometry_test.gd
```

174 s and 198 assertions at the default four cycles; 515 s and 872 assertions at
three times that (12 cycles, 72 rest-state snapshots, 48 leak watches, 12
destruction/regeneration lifecycles of which 6 were losses in open space, 12
whole-`Main` re-entries, 12 cabin-and-bunk round trips, 0 failures). The suite
prints one `PHANTOM_CYCLE` JSON line per rest state per cycle, one
`PHANTOM_LEAK` line per lifecycle watched inside a cycle, and one
`PHANTOM_SUMMARY`.

## What is checked

Six rest states are reached every cycle, each of them from a staged, repeatable
viewpoint — the production player spawn on foot, or the craft's own berth's
published assist-capture pose in flight — because several craft swap LOD
renderers by camera distance and two cycles photographed from two places would
differ for a legitimate reason:

| state | how it is reached |
| --- | --- |
| `on_foot_yard` | player on foot at the yard, nothing running |
| `activity_abandoned` | the station-perimeter nearby-sector activity started and abandoned through the production HUD intents |
| `cabin_bunk_returned` | back in the pilot seat after leaving it into the in-flight cabin, sleeping in a physical bunk, waking and retaking the seat |
| `craft_parked` | the craft flown, landed through the real landing assist and left — on alternate cycles, lost in flight and regenerated by its berth instead |
| `destroyed_regenerated` | a parked craft damaged to critical, destroyed, and regenerated at its berth; rotating across all nine flyable craft |
| `after_reentry` | a real settings/session save and a whole-`Main` detach and re-entry |

At each of them the suite walks the live tree **from the scene-tree root** —
`HeroDamagePresentation` detaches its destruction effects into the tree root by
design, so a walk rooted at `Main` would miss exactly the class of phantom this
audit is for — and records:

* every `GeometryInstance3D` with `is_visible_in_tree()`, with its AABB,
  `cast_shadow`, `transparency`, and, for particle renderers, `emitting`;
* every `Light3D` with `visible_in_tree`, its energy, and its per-tick energy
  series over two simulated seconds;
* the text of every world `Label3D`, because a display's AABB is a function of
  the string it is showing;
* each `ShipBerthFeedback`'s drawn lease glyphs against the state it publishes.

Renderer AABBs are taken in the frame of the nearest owning `HeroShip` where
there is one and in world space otherwise, so a craft holding station is not
confused with a presentation stranded where a craft was destroyed — that one has
no ship to be carried by.

The central contract is a **cycle-to-cycle diff of the same state**: after the
warm-up window the visible renderer set at a state may not gain a member, no
renderer that held still may have moved, no steady light may have changed energy
and no world display may have changed text. That needs no hand-authored
inventory of what the game draws. Four absolute contracts back it up, because a
phantom already present in the warm-up cycle would diff clean against itself: no
transient damage/destruction/repair/entry renderer drawn at rest, no particle
system emitting at rest, no light outside the reduced-flash contract, and one
lease-glyph state per berth. Three **same-cycle leak watches** bracket the
activity, the whole sortie and the destruction/regeneration with a before/after
pair, which catches a leak the cross-cycle diff cannot see because it was
already there last cycle.

### The flash thresholds are read off the production scripts

* Every light a production script declares `reduced_flash_safe` on is declared
  so on the stated grounds that it has no animation or process path
  (`scripts/world/habitat_spine.gd`, `scripts/world/aft_junction_stack.gd`,
  `scripts/world/ship_berth_feedback.gd`,
  `scripts/ships/jovian_engineer_repair_presentation.gd`), matching the
  `steady_no_flash` policy `scripts/ui/caption_presenter.gd`,
  `scripts/ui/caption_accessibility_contract.gd` and `scripts/ui/hud.gd`
  publish. Such a light modulating at all fails its own declaration.
* The only ambient light modulation the station authors is
  `ShipyardWorld._animate_warning_lights`: `base * (0.35 + 0.65 * max(0,
  sin(elapsed * 3.8)))`. 3.8 rad/s is **0.605 Hz** at a modulation depth of
  **0.65** of peak. That is the authored ceiling for a light allowed to move
  while the world is at rest, and the suite uses it, one estimator step wide.
* The only production lights authored to modulate faster are
  `HeroDamagePresentation`'s `DamageWarningLight` (`sin(elapsed * 13)`, 2.07 Hz,
  and `sin(elapsed * 7)`, 1.11 Hz, below critical) and `EngineFailureLight`
  (`sin(elapsed * 29) + sin(elapsed * 61)`, 4.6 and 9.7 Hz) — each from zero, so
  at full depth. All of them belong to a damage lifecycle that has to be over
  before the craft is at rest, and all of them are separated from the station
  ambient by the ceiling above.

Measured across every rest state: 42 of the 280 visible lights modulate, all of
them the authored station warning practicals, all at 0.25–0.75 Hz measured
(0.605 Hz authored, quantised by the two-second window) at depth 0.65. Zero
lights over the contract, zero `reduced_flash_safe` lights modulating, zero
lifecycle-presentation lights modulating.

## What the instrument proved it can see

The first run of the suite, before its rest states were separated from the
damage lifecycle, reported
`HalyardCrewTransport/HeroDamagePresentation/DamageSparks` visible **and
emitting** on a craft parked at its berth with the player on foot — the exact
shape of the recorded defect. That turned out to be correct production
behaviour (a hull damaged to 65 % sparks whether powered or not; the suite's own
loop had left it damaged), and the loop was changed so every rest state is
reached on a nominal craft. It is recorded here because it is the evidence that
the audit would catch the original defect if it came back.

The suite also asserts, inside the destruction leg, that the doomed craft's
damage channels are actually *raised* before it is destroyed and *cleared* after
it regenerates. A "nothing is drawn at rest" pass means nothing if nothing could
ever have been drawn.

## Found and fixed

**`scripts/ships/halyard_crew_transport.gd` — a regenerated or re-entered
Halyard came back with the repair-kit inventory missing from its cabin panel.**

*Symptom.* The player lands the Halyard, walks out, and later walks back into
its cabin. The engineer panel on the cabin status bulkhead reads
`REPAIR [READY // IDLE // REPAIR READY]` — the `// KITS 6/6` field simply gone,
on a craft carrying a full kit load. The same craft parked in the same state
after being lost in flight and regenerated at its berth reads
`REPAIR [READY // IDLE // REPAIR READY // KITS 6/6]`. Same craft, same berth,
same empty roster, two different panels, and the field only comes back when some
later crew or repair event happens to republish it.

*Cause.* `_exit_tree()` calls `_clear_engineer_status_display_lifecycle()`,
which parks the panel on `HalyardCrewStatusDisplay.clear_for_detach()`'s
detached copy (repair view zeroed, so `resource_capacity == 0` and
`_repair_token()` emits no kit segment) and arms the one-shot
`_skip_next_crew_status_repair_snapshot`. That skip belongs to the detach: it
keeps the outgoing lifecycle's repair envelope off a panel that is going away.
Left armed across the re-entry, it was consumed instead by the
`call_deferred("refresh_crew_status_display")` that `_enter_tree()` posts to
restore the craft — so the restoring refresh published no envelope at all and
re-rendered the cleared view. Every production `Main` re-entry streams the craft
out and back, so this is the state a saved and resumed session leaves the panel
in. The reuse/regeneration path happened to escape it because
`_commit_variant_reset_for_reuse` ends in a second, unconditional refresh.

*Fix.* Disarm the detach's one-shot skip on the way back into the tree, so the
restoring refresh is the one that republishes the live repair snapshot. One line
in `_enter_tree()`.

*Focused assertion.* `tests/halyard_crew_status_display_integration_test.gd`
now streams the craft out of the tree and back and requires the panel to come
back carrying the craft's full kit capacity. The assertion is red without the
fix and green with it.

## Accepted, with reasons

| observation | why it is not a defect |
| --- | --- |
| 42 station warning practicals pulse at 0.605 Hz, depth 0.65, at every rest state | authored ambient beacons in `ShipyardWorld._animate_warning_lights`; they are the contract's own reference point, and at 0.605 Hz nothing about them approaches a flash hazard |
| `Zenith`/`Torrent` `LOD0`/`LOD1` groups swap membership between two walks | `ZenithAuthoredPresentation._apply_lod` selects one group by camera distance with authored hysteresis, so which is drawn depends on where the camera came from, not on any lifecycle. Membership of these groups is excluded from the set diffs and reported on its own line; everything else about them — transient names, emission, position, flicker inside a rest window — is audited normally |
| ~241 renderers move inside every rest window | patrolling service drones, the sweeping sensor masts, the traffic plot arm, the crane. Authored animation with no resting pose, so they are excluded from the cycle-to-cycle position contract and reported as a count |
| the Halyard's crew readout `Label3D` changes AABB once in the life of the process | its `_ready()` placeholder roster is replaced by live text the first time a pilot is aboard. This is why the warm-up window is two cycles, exactly as the long-session soak warms up for four |
| a craft damaged and not repaired keeps `DamageSparks` emitting while parked and powered down | `HeroDamagePresentation._apply_stage_visuals` raises the hull channel on `visible_damage`, which is true for any state but `hidden`. Battle damage on a parked hull is authored, not dormant geometry |

## Not covered here

* **The three runtime-composed Cinder craft carry no `HeroDamagePresentation`
  at all.** Only the six craft with a `scenes/ships/*.tscn` instance have one, so
  `cinder_cargo_hauler`, `cinder_light_interceptor` and
  `cinder_long_range_bomber` raise none of the shared impact, spark, smoke,
  warning-light or destruction channels when they are damaged or destroyed; they
  carry only their own bespoke cues (the interceptor's engine damage beacon, the
  hauler's cargo threshold light). The suite records them in
  `craft_without_damage_presentation` and skips the raised-channel assertion for
  them rather than passing it vacuously. This is a *missing* presentation, not a
  phantom, and it belongs to the fleet presentation-coverage item.
* **Atmospheric entry-heat overlays and the Jovian engineer repair-arc cues.**
  Their transient renderer names are in the audited set, so one left drawn at a
  yard rest state would fail — but neither lifecycle is *driven* by this loop
  (entry heat needs a planetary descent, the repair arc needs a bound engineer
  running a repair). Driving them belongs to the planetary and crew-role suites.
* **Off-spawn viewpoints.** The rest snapshots are taken from one staged
  viewpoint per state so the visible set is reproducible. A renderer that is
  only ever drawn from somewhere else is outside what this suite compares,
  though it is still subject to the absolute transient/emission/light contracts,
  which are camera-independent.
* **The activity roster's own presentation.** The nearby-sector activity is
  started and abandoned every cycle and the leak watch around it is clean, but
  the encounter's hostile roster is not flown against; a phantom that only
  appears after a *completed* encounter is not reached.
